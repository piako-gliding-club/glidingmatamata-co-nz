Gliders depend on the weather. They need either a strong windy day or a hot sunny day. We tend to avoid rainy days, or still days with no sun.

Most gliders don't have engines, so they're towed up behind a tow plane to a chosen release height. Some have a small built-in engine they use to launch. We also have a winch, which can launch a glider from the ground.

Gliders come with one seat or two. We mainly use the two-seaters for training and trial flights. You can see the club's gliders on [our aircraft](/aircraft/) page.

## How do gliders stay up?

On sunny days [thermals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal) usually form, and we fly from one thermal to the next. The sun heats the ground, which heats the air above it, and that hot air rises in invisible columns. We circle inside a column, and if it's strong enough we climb higher. A thermal is often marked by a small fluffy cloud on top, so we fly from one cloud to the next.

In a strong westerly we can use [ridge lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_lift) to fly up and down the Kaimai Range. In a strong westerly or easterly we may also fly in the [lee waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_wave) that form off the Kaimais.

## How far can gliders travel?

On hot, sunny thermal days we can fly hundreds of kilometres across the country, often to Auckland, Thames, Tokoroa, Te Kuiti, Taupō or further. Members of the Taupō, Auckland and Tauranga clubs often fly to Matamata, and we fly to them.

## How fast can gliders go?

Most gliders cruise at an airspeed of 40–60 knots (74–111 km/h), and have a maximum airspeed of around 130 knots (240 km/h). Each type of glider has its own speed limits. Airspeed is the speed through the air, as opposed to the speed across the ground.

## Can gliders do aerobatics?

Yes. Most gliders can do loops, spins, stalls and chandelles, and some can even fly inverted.

## Gliding competitions

In summer we hold gliding competitions that can last up to a week. Up to 30 gliders can be flying at once. Each day pilots are set a task: to reach several turnpoints scattered across the countryside, as fast as they can.

Closer to home, members fly our [club tasks](/club-tasks/) through the season, and the best flights win [club trophies](/trophies/).

description: How gliding training works at Piako Gliding Club, from a first lesson to solo and beyond.
parent: /trial-flights
intro: Most pilots start with a trial lesson to see if they enjoy it — no commitment to a full course. From there, training is a personal path with no fixed timeline.
journey_image: /images/soaring-journey.webp
journey_text: For some pilots a solo licence is a sufficient goal in itself, whilst others will continue to progress through soaring, cross country flying, racing and ultimately, for a very select few, national representation in international competition.
stages:
  - title: First lesson
    text: Your first flight with an instructor, no commitment required. Grants temporary club membership so you can keep flying at club rates.
  - title: Solo pilot
    text: Structured ab-initio training builds toward your first solo flight, typically after 20–30 flights, with a simple GP medical declaration.
  - title: Soaring pilot
    text: Beyond solo, you learn to use rising air to stay up for longer and extend your range around the local area.
  - title: Cross country pilot
    text: Fly beyond gliding range of the airfield, planning routes and landing options across the countryside.
  - title: Task pilot
    text: Fly set tasks against the clock, the skills that underpin club and national competition flying.
  - title: Alpine pilot
    text: Fly the mountains, using wave and ridge lift for some of the most spectacular flying in the country.
how_it_works:
  - title: Who can fly
    text: Gliding is an aviation sport that is open to most people, including certain disabilities.
  - title: Learning with an instructor
    text: New pilots, ab initio (from the beginning) students, fly with an instructor in a two-seat training glider. A specific syllabus is followed that develops the new pilot's skills in a structured manner. Progress is recorded in a personal flying logbook and student progress card.
  - title: Medical
    text: Before flying solo a medical declaration or certificate is required. This is very straightforward and can be signed by the pilot's doctor. No medical is needed for a trial flight.
  - title: How long to go solo
    text: Experience has shown that even the most adept students require at least 20 to 30 flights to achieve solo status. It is not unusual for slower students to need much more than this.
  - title: Starting out
    text: The majority of students start with a trial lesson to see if they enjoy it. There is no commitment to a full course, and a trial flight gives temporary membership rights for a limited period. This enables flying at club rates during this trial period if desired.
  - title: A typical lesson
    text: Instruction is normally one flight from aerotow (launch is being towed by a light aircraft), which can be up to one hour if soaring conditions are present, or about 15 minutes if the air is still. We also offer winch launches, which are about 5 minutes if no soaring is possible.

    text: All our flying costs are on the [fees & prices page](/fees/).

bring:
  - Sunglasses
  - A hat or cap if you have one
  - Sunscreen. You'll be outside most of the time, and it's essential in summer
  - A jacket and warm clothes. The airfield is exposed, and often windy and cold while waiting on the ground
  - Lunch and water. You'll likely spend the day parked at the end of the airfield
  - A camera. You can take photos while up in the glider
  - Cash or EFTPOS card preferred. There is a surcharge for credit cards at the airfield
  - Your trial flight voucher, if you have one
reassurance: Yes — if you want to. That's the point. The controls are offered, never forced. There's no fitness test, nothing to study, and no obligation to carry on afterwards. Your instructor handles takeoff, landing, and anything you're not comfortable with.
weather: Gliding depends on the weather. If your day looks unsuitable we'll contact you to reschedule at no charge. Facing a long drive and unsure? Phone the day's instructor the night before, or check the [webcams and forecasts](/weather/).
faqs:
  - question: Do I need any experience?
    answer: None at all. Ab-initio students start from scratch. The instructor follows a structured syllabus and guides you through everything on the day.
  - question: Do gift vouchers expire?
    answer: Vouchers are valid for 3 years, and we still honour expired ones. If prices have gone up since you bought it, you may need to pay the difference. Contact us to confirm.
  - question: Is there a weight or age limit?
    answer: Our gliders have a seat limit of 110kg. There is no minimum weight in the two-seat gliders. Gliding is suitable for ages 8 to 85 — getting in a glider is a bit like getting in the bath.
  - question: Do I need a medical?
    answer: "There are no medical requirements for flying with an instructor. Solo flight requires a medical declaration from your GP which is no more stringent than the requirements for a driver's licence. If you're thinking of taking up gliding, you can see the medical form among the [Gliding New Zealand operational forms ↗](gnz-ops-forms)."
  - question: What should I wear or bring?
    answer: Flat shoes, sunglasses, a hat and a light jacket — plus sunscreen in summer. We provide everything else you'll need in the glider.
  - question: How long will the visit take?
    answer: Gliding is a day at the airfield — volunteers must inspect aircraft, organise launch order, and plan around the weather. It's worth settling in and enjoying it. For a trial flight, allow at least half a day to avoid disappointment.
  - question: Can I bring someone to watch?
    answer: Absolutely. Family and friends are welcome to come along and watch from the launch point — it's part of the day.
---

Turn up a bit before your booking if you can, and allow an hour or so for your flight. Gliding runs on the weather and delays are common, so we suggest keeping half a day free so you're not rushed or disappointed. How long you're in the air depends on which flight you booked.

Look for our club caravan. It will be at one of 4 points on the airfield. It's OK to drive on the airfield to get to us: keep your hazard lights on, stick well to the side, and give way to all aircraft, both on the ground and coming in to land.

The club rooms have tea and coffee facilities and toilets. Food is available from Waharoa, about 5 minutes drive south of the field. The nearest ATM is in Matamata.

If a friend or family member would also like a flight, we may be able to fit them in on the day, but it's best to book them ahead so we can plan for it.
